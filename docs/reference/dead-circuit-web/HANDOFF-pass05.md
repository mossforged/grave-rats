# Dead Circuit: continuation guide

## User goal and decisions

The user imagined a nearly top-down sci-fi shooter inside derelict ships: connected rooms and corridors with FPS-style alternate routes, scavenged equipment/ammo, monsters or robots, and possible co-op/competitive play. The agreed prototype focus is a handcrafted solo mission, with co-op as a future validation step. The user values matching the supplied concept art, especially perspective, industrial interiors, and the character's visual quality.

Pass 04 identified the production gap: procedural boxes and code-built characters could not reach the concept's finished asset quality. Pass 05 (this package) built the asset pipeline to close it. No paid assets were bought and no third-party models are used: every mesh, rig, clip and texture is generated by scripts in `game/assets-src/` and is the user's own.

## Current state (pass 05)

The playable browser game is still Three.js 0.170.0 with buildless ES modules and static HTML/CSS, and the same single solo mission. What changed is how it looks and how the assets are made:

- **Characters:** `operator.glb` and `robot.glb` are skinned glTF characters built in Blender 5.2 (headless, via `bpy`) on one shared 22-bone skeleton. There are 13 clips. Lower-body clips are `L_Idle`, `L_Run_F/B`, `L_Strafe_L/R`, `L_Walk_F`, `L_Crouch_Idle` and `L_Crouch_Walk`. Upper-body clips are `U_Aim`, `U_LowReady` and `U_Reload`; the reload has a real magazine bone, so the mag is pulled, stowed, swapped and reseated. Full-body clips are `F_Roll` and `F_Death`. The hands are held on the weapon with IK during authoring, then baked to plain FK keys, so the engine only plays rotations. Clips are keyed poses: authored in code rather than mocap or hand-animated in a DCC UI.
- **Animation controller (`dist/src/characters.js`):** four directional run clips blend by the move direction relative to aim, so strafing and back-pedalling look right while aiming. Lower- and upper-body layers are filtered by bone, a full-body layer overrides for roll and death, and procedural recoil and flinch are added after the mixer.
- **Environment kit (`kit.glb`):** 32 bevelled modular pieces. Walls that face the camera are full height (2.6 m) with light-strip, pipe, vent, screen, medbay and medbay-sign variants. Walls between the camera and a room are cut away low (0.78 m) with a thick dark top, as in the concept's dollhouse view. Pilasters sit on every wall vertex. There are floor plates, grate tiles, bulkhead frames with sliding leaves, and medbay and cargo props.
- **Materials:** 15 tileable texture sets (`dist/assets/tex/`, generated by `assets-src/textures.py`). Each set's albedo, tangent normal and ORM (AO, roughness, metalness) come from one authored height and wear stack, so they agree with each other. Pass 04 reused colour maps as bump maps; that is gone. `dist/src/materials.js` binds sets to glTF slots by material name.
- **Lighting and post:**
  - Per-room practical point lights hung at 4.6 m (lower, they blew out helmets): cool medbay, cool corridor, sodium-amber cargo, cyan engineering.
  - A shadow-casting weapon light at the muzzle.
  - A sun shadow frustum that follows the camera.
  - RoomEnvironment reflections.
  - Post chain: GTAO ambient occlusion, then bloom with an HDR knee at 2.8, so only emitters, muzzle flashes and tracers bloom; then output.
  - `?q=low` skips the post chain.
- **Camera:** orthographic, offset (12,40,29) as before. Default view span is 17 (was 29) and the wheel range is 12–36.

### Polished vs. legacy areas

The medbay, the corridor junction and the cargo hold are the authored slice: kit props, wall-hugging dressing, blood and grime smears, stencils, dedicated lights. Every room uses the new floors, walls, posts, doors, pickups and characters. Engineering's reactor and pumps, the maintenance shaft racks, the airlock lockers and the crew-quarters bunks are still pass-04 procedural geometry, now wearing the new PBR materials. They are the next candidates for kit pieces.

## Gameplay-visible changes in pass 05 (everything else unchanged)

1. **Closed bulkheads now seal their corridor.** In pass 04 each door leaf was 4.35 m wide inside a 6.6 m opening, so the player could walk around a closed door through about 1.1 m gaps. The new frames fill the opening and their jambs are solids.
2. **New props with collision footprints:** three medbay cabinets, two medbay canisters, a medbay gurney, two cargo shelves, four cargo crates and a canister, and two corridor canisters. All hug walls and no existing route is blocked (the travel test still passes). They do add cover points.
3. **Default zoom is tighter** (span 17) so the operator reads at roughly the concept's scale.
4. **Robot tracers** start at the robot's muzzle. This is visual only.
5. **The roll is now an authored clip.** Timing, speed, cooldown, dodge window and firing lockout are unchanged.

All pass-04 collision footprints for existing props are unchanged. Pass-05 kit props are scaled to those footprints.

## Running and editing

Use `game/` as the project root. `dist/` is the shipped site: `game.js` (world, simulation, input, UI binding), `src/materials.js`, `src/characters.js`, `assets/`, `vendor/` (Three.js core, the add-ons used, fonts), and `dev/viewer.html` (character and clip viewer). An import map in `index.html` maps `three` to `vendor/three.module.js`. Asset references use `?v=5`.

Rebuild the assets with `npm run assets`. That needs Python 3.13 and `pip install bpy numpy scipy pillow`; bpy is Blender 5.2 as a Python module, about 400 MB. `build_characters.py` exits with an error if any roll frame pushes the body or a weapon through the deck. Blender sources are saved to `assets-src/characters.blend` as a by-product. The kit is fully reproducible from script.

## World and rendering facts

- A 43 × 36 grid with tile 2.2 m and 899 walkable cells; rooms and corridors are as in pass 04.
- Visibility shading tints floor and wall instances: visible 1.0, explored 0.34, unexplored 0.1. Enemies and pickups hide outside sight as before. Props and walls in unexplored rooms are still drawn (dimmed), so this is not complete fog of war.
- Draw calls measured in the headless capture are roughly 750–850 per frame. That includes two shadow passes (sun and weapon light) and the GTAO normal pass. Real-GPU frame time has not been measured; the capture machine uses software GL. `?q=low` removes about half the passes.

## Controls and mechanics

Unchanged from pass 04: WASD/arrows, Shift run, mouse aim/fire, 1/2 weapons, R reload, Q medkit, F light, E interact, M map, Esc pause, Space roll, hold C crouch. Recover the recorder at tile (25,23) and extract at the airlock (5.5,27.5).

## Roll implementation and limits

The gameplay roll is unchanged: 0.62 s, 7.8 m/s, 1.7 s cooldown, dodge window 0.16–0.52 s, reload cancelled, no firing. The visual is now the `F_Roll` clip:

- Dip, then push-off with the legs extended behind, then a tuck.
- The pelvis follows a raised arc through the 360° turn while the rifle is held tight against the right flank.
- Feet are planted with leg IK at take-off and landing.

The Blender build validates every baked frame against the deck, body and both weapons. The in-engine test samples skinned vertices per frame; the worst is −1.3 cm, inside the pass-04 tolerance of −2.5 cm. The residual is linear interpolation between baked frames cutting the arc of a planted foot. Weapons were the hard case: holding the rifle on the chest drove the barrel up to 36 cm into the deck mid-roll. The old test missed this because three.js caches skinned bounding boxes; the new test measures per vertex.

## Cover: important accuracy (unchanged)

There are NO dedicated snap-to-cover, leaning, peeking, cover-height or vault systems. Holding C slows movement and disables firing, and the crouch clips now show it. `coverProtects` checks whether a nearby solid lies toward the firing enemy. The HUD says COVER NEARBY based on proximity only. Solids block sight and bullets as 2D footprints even while standing, and height is not modelled. The new props add more of these 2D footprints; they do not make cover more sophisticated. Next step: low/tall cover metadata with height-aware sight and projectile checks. The kit's crate heights (0.7–1.7 m) are a natural source for that.

## Weapon feedback

As in pass 04: hitscan, visual tracers, muzzle burst, brass, smoke, recoil, camera kick and hit markers. Muzzle position now comes from a socket on the weapon bone, and the reload clip performs the magazine swap. Flash, tracer and spark materials are HDR so they bloom. Transient effect geometry and material disposal still needs attention under sustained fire.

## Verification and its limits

`check-engine.cjs` runs **93 assertions**: the original 81, unchanged, plus 12 for pass 05.

- Skinned assets load and all 13 clips bind.
- Standing feet are on the deck.
- Skinned roll clearance is measured per vertex.
- The roll lowers the pelvis and returns to locomotion.
- The muzzle sits at the weapon tip.
- The shotgun mesh toggles on weapon swap.
- The reload clip blends in.
- A closed bulkhead seals its side gaps; an open one is passable.
- The robot death clip drops the body and kills its eye light.

There were no page errors.

`qa.cjs` is a UI smoke test. `shots.cjs` renders fixed views for visual comparison; it is not an assertion suite. None of this proves combat balance, real-GPU performance, animation feel at speed, or multiplayer readiness.

## Remaining gaps against the concept (candid)

1. **Room scale.** Gameplay rooms are 2–3× larger than the concept's, so the medbay and cargo floors read emptier even with dressing. Shrinking `T` or the room rectangles is a level-design decision that changes routes; it was not made here.
2. **Character fidelity.** The operator is a clean, segmented hard-surface suit, not a sculpted model with fabric folds, decals or a baked high-to-low normal map. The next step is sculpting and baking in Blender, or commissioning or buying a character with the user's approval. The skeleton, bone names and clip names are the integration contract, so any replacement rigged to them plugs straight in.
3. **Animation quality.** Clips are keyed poses with eased interpolation. They read correctly from the game camera but lack the weight, overlap and secondary motion of mocap or hand-polished animation. Roll, reload and death are the most worth an animator's pass.
4. **Texture authorship.** The maps are procedurally authored and coherent, but they are not photoscanned or hand-painted, and tiling repeats are visible on large floors. Decals help but there are few of them.
5. **No ceiling or volumetrics.** The concept's light shafts and haze are only approximated by a beam mesh and dust motes.
6. **Legacy rooms** (see above).
7. **Cover** (see above).

## Hosting and credentials

The original site is a ChatGPT Sites project (`deployment/original-site.json`; owner-private at last verification). That host is not reachable from this continuation, so **pass 05 has not been deployed**: the published site still runs pass 04, and nothing was published elsewhere or had its access changed. Any static host can serve `game/dist`; `.glb` needs the `model/gltf-binary` MIME type, and `server.mjs` now sends it. No credentials are included.
