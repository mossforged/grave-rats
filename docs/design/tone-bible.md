# Tone bible (lead draft, 2026-10-29; John edits freely)

**The joke:** the future's dumbest economics kill you, and the crew does it anyway because rent is due. Played straight inside the ship, funny everywhere around it.

## Where the humour lives (and where it doesn't)
- **Around the run:** contracts, briefings, the end-of-shift payslip, equipment rental, the death bill, the company voice in your helmet.
- **In the details:** stencils, labels and posters aboard the derelicts; the dead crew's notes; what the rival pirates have painted on their suits.
- **Not in the fights.** When the acid-spitter comes through the door, nobody is quipping. The violence lands.

## The world (placeholder names, all invented)
- **The Drift:** the graveyard orbit where dead ships end up after salvage law, bankruptcies and "incidents".
- **Your employer:** a salvage liquidator, working name *Remnant Asset Recovery*, motto "Everything Has a Second Owner". You are not an employee; you're an "independent recovery contractor".
- **Contracts** are written like terms of service. "The vessel is believed to be **probably abandoned**." "Contractor assumes all liability for anything that is not abandoned."
- **Rival crews** are better funded and worse people: venture-backed raider outfits with sponsor decals, quarterly kill targets and a brand voice.
- **The threats** (rogue AI, robots, aliens, horrors) are taken seriously. The satire is about the people who send you in, not the things that eat you.

## Running gags (use sparingly; each lands three times, not thirty)
- **Fees.** Airlock cycling fee. Oxygen convenience fee. A posthumous processing fee charged to your next of kin, or to your next clone if we go that way.
- **"Probably abandoned."** Every briefing says it, and the evidence otherwise keeps piling up.
- **Payslips.** The end-of-shift screen itemises what you earned and then what the company deducted: suit rental, ammunition at company-store prices, "morale".
- **Ship's AI politeness.** A hostile ship AI that still follows customer-service scripts while venting your room.
- **Safety signage.** Increasingly useless warnings in deadpan corporate pictograms.

## Voice rules
- Deadpan beats wacky. The company never thinks it's being funny.
- Short. A contract clause, a sign, a line on a payslip: never a paragraph.
- Punch up. The targets are companies, fees and fine print, not the crew's desperation itself.
- No real brands, politicians or celebrities. Invented everything.
