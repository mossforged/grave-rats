# Make greed visible, keep danger honest

Grave Rats is building on ground that comparable games have already mapped, and they point the same way. **The co-op hits for friend groups (Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O.) won with a short dive on a visible clock, a shared quota, almost no persistent loss, and comedy that comes from the system and the players instead of from written jokes.** The horror shooters that lean on darkness and limited sight (Shadowgrounds, Darkwood, Teleglitch, Alien Breed Evolution) show that a steep camera and a forward cone produce close-range dread almost for free. They also show the most common complaint: darkness or the camera hiding the objective, the route, or whatever just killed you. On combat, the clearest lesson comes from Resident Evil 4, F.E.A.R., Alien: Isolation and Left 4 Dead. Deliberate controls only feel good when enemies are designed around them, when every limit can be seen, and when threats have to back off on a schedule. On tone, the satire that lasts is satire built into the rules (Hardspace's debt and paid-for death, Lethal's quota). Satire delivered by talking characters (High on Life, Deep Rock Galactic's Scout barks, Borderlands) wears out quickly. On multiplayer, PvPvE against strangers has hurt or killed studios far larger than a hobby team, mostly through cheating and the need for a big enough player population. A host-authoritative listen server over GodotSteam, structured now and networked later, is the realistic path for four friends. The rest of this report covers the five areas you asked about. Each section separates what the sources say (with citations) from what it means for Grave Rats (marked as inference). It finishes with recommendations tied to where the project stands now: the grey-box core loop has passed, stage 3 art is next, and co-op comes later.

## Presentation: a steep camera gives you dread for free and blame for nothing

### What the sources show

The fixed, high camera with limited sight distance gets consistent praise for close-range tension. A retrospective of **Shadowgrounds** notes that because the camera shows only what is in front of the character, "all encounters happen at close range" and enemies are "already too close for comfort". The shoulder flashlight's shadows "form and pool again as soon as the beam moves past", and the writer thought this would be "much less impressive in first-person". The same writer admits spending a while trying to lower the camera before accepting it couldn't be done ([Waltorious](https://waltoriouswritesaboutgames.com/2015/09/27/history-lessons-shadowgrounds/)). **Darkwood's** three-person team found the horror in their field-of-view system only by playtesting what started as a tower-defence prototype. Their stated aim was "seeing (or hearing) objects move without knowing what caused the motion", and they called overused jump scares "shallow" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-darkwood-i-s-visibility-mechanics-create-a-new-kind-of-horror)). **Teleglitch** shows only what is in line of sight and reveals level geometry from the shadows as you move, like Monaco. Critics praised it, though many complaints focused on its difficulty ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleglitch)).

The criticism is just as consistent. **Alien Breed Evolution** is the closest analogue: top-down, set on a derelict ship, full of aliens. GameSpot found some areas so dim that required windows and explosive canisters were easy to miss, sometimes hidden by the camera angle as well. Keycards, which glow, were easy to find. The reviewer also objected that camera rotation was locked in co-op, and said the minimap-as-motion-tracker didn't always make the route clear ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/alien-breed-evolution-review/1900-6244192/)). In **Intravenous**, light was a readable stealth mechanic, but enemies could "spot and fire on you whilst they themselves are out of *your* field of view" ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/intravenous-review-splinter-cell-miami/)). **The Ascent** was called "the best-looking isometric viewing experience", yet high-level enemies one-shot the reviewer through a fence before they noticed, and the navigation line led into high-level zones ([Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/reviews/review-the-ascent)). Darkwood's critics flagged that key threat information comes through audio with no visual backup, which shuts out deaf and hard-of-hearing players. They also flagged a palette so desaturated it is "almost black and white", and no colourblind modes ([GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/daniel-weissenberger/darkwood-review/)). At the other extreme, **Ruiner's** cable clutter, warning stripes, giant red text and strobing objectives added up to sensory overload ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/ruiner-review)), and **Synthetik's** tiny, non-resizable text was its clearest weakness ([GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/brad-gallaway/synthetik-ultimate-review/)).

On technique, Teleglitch fakes its view cones by **extruding black polygons from walls away from the player**: "not 100% correct, but definitely good enough". The shadows start slightly below the tops of objects, so the tops stay visible ([simonschreibt.de](https://simonschreibt.de/?p=66)). For walls that block the view of the player, the best-documented method is **Baldur's Gate 3's occlusion cutout**: a sphere mask between character and camera with a noise-broken edge, through which mouse clicks pass so players can path to revealed spots. An artist has recreated it and notes it is doable in Godot ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/artist-recreated-baldur-s-gate-3-occlusion-cutout-effect)). **Diablo IV** tested a closer third-person camera and went back to isometric because it "felt like Diablo". Its camera pulls in close in towns and out for world bosses to fit more players ([Gameswelt via VG247](https://www.gameswelt.de/diablo-iv/news/diese-krasse-aenderung-hat-sich-nicht-durchgesetzt-299772)). It also ships a black-point and brightness calibration screen and uses emissive glow on interactables ([Blizzard graphics blog](https://news.blizzard.com/fr-fr/article/23964183/sous-le-vernis-les-graphismes-de-diablo-iv)).

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

The ~60° camera with flashlight-as-sight already has the Shadowgrounds and Darkwood effect. The design risk now is fairness, not atmosphere. Four rules follow from the evidence. First, **anything the player needs (salvage, doors, extraction, hazards) gets an emissive or rim treatment that reads outside the cone**, the Alien Breed keycard lesson. Second, **nothing should hurt the player without revealing itself**. Pirate muzzle flash should light the shooter. Hits should show a direction indicator and be colour-coded (acid versus bullets). Ranged enemies should ideally need to see a lit player before they can engage. Third, **every audio-only cue needs a visual twin**. A motion-tracker blip in the Alien Breed style is the genre-appropriate one, because it adds information without lighting the scene. Fourth, **draw remembered geometry dim and desaturated and live sight in full colour**, and leave a faint last-known marker or the top of a silhouette at the edge of sight, Teleglitch's partial-reveal trick. That cuts down "hidden enemy" complaints without removing dread. For walls, a permanent low-stub cut-away on camera-facing walls is cheaper and easier to read than per-frame dithering. Reserve a BG3-style noise mask for tall props, make aim and interaction raycasts ignore cut geometry, and let cut walls keep casting full-height shadows so the cone still respects them. Expect new players to fight the camera at first, as the Shadowgrounds writer did, and teach it in the first minute. If any zoom or rotation exists, don't lock it in co-op. A Diablo-style pull-back when the crew spreads out or a big fight starts is a proven pattern.

## Mechanics: weight only works when enemies are designed around it

### What the sources show

Resident Evil 4 is the best-documented case of deliberate controls creating tension instead of frustration. It kept stop-and-shoot because moving while shooting "would enable circle-strafing and make enemies easy unless their AI were retuned". Its enemies rush, then **shuffle in and surround**, rather than out-manoeuvring the player ([Game Developer: Scare Tactics](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/in-depth-scare-tactics---how-horror-games-have-evolved-their-controls)). The same analysis notes that tank controls, "commonly believed" to simulate panic, were simply frustrating. Disorienting controls are not weighty ones. It also notes that Dead Space's move-and-shoot traded dread for pace. RE4's laser dot makes precision visible, and its **location-specific hit reactions** (leg shot kneels, shoulder spins, head staggers) are consistent enough to be crowd control. Its kick is offered only on staggered enemies ([Tilting at Pixels](https://tiltingatpixels.com/post/Resident-Evil-4-Retrospective/)). On input handling, cancel and buffer design "largely determines whether a game feels crisp or laggy". God of War allows defensive cancels before the hit, while heavy moves can only be cancelled after the hit ([Game Developer: Combat Canceled](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/combat-canceled-i-god-of-war-i-action-game-design)). Over-generous buffering in No Rest for the Wicked queued several rolls and weapon swaps after players had changed their minds, and players asked for a shorter buffer ([NRftW forum](https://forum.norestforthewicked.com/t/input-buffering-combat-queueing-feels-too-unresponsive-in-dynamic-situations/16313)). Helldivers 2 treats handling weight as a **per-weapon "Ergonomics" stat** that bulky attachments lower, which turns heaviness into a loadout trade-off. This comes from a secondary guide, with no official numbers ([GamesHorizon](https://gameshorizon.com/guides/ergonomics-in-helldivers-2-explained/)).

For threats, **Alien: Isolation** separates a director that knows where the player is but "never tells the alien exactly where you are", only the general area, from a xenomorph that must find you with its own senses. A menace gauge rises with proximity and with the player seeing or hearing it. At the peak, the alien retreats "backstage". Gunfire is loud, the flashlight is noticed almost immediately, and it can attack only after visual confirmation or touch. Behaviours such as searching lockers unlock only after the player has escaped that way several times, which looks like learning ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)). **Left 4 Dead's** director tracks intensity per survivor, acts on the maximum, and cycles Build Up, then Sustain Peak (3–5 s), then Peak Fade, then Relax (30–45 s). Specials spawn only out of sight. The lessons are "adjust frequency, not difficulty", and scripted placement gets memorised ([Booth, Valve 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). **Lethal Company** gives each monster one learnable rule and a sensory tell: one freezes when looked at, one is blind and hunts by sound, the Jester's music box warns you ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/lethal-company/monsters), a guide site).

For human squads, **F.E.A.R.'s** celebrated AI ran at most one squad behaviour at a time (Get-to-Cover, Advance-Cover, Orderly-Advance, Search). One member suppressed while the others moved, and the squad never assigned two AIs to the same cover node. "We actually did not have any complex squad behaviors at all". Flanking emerged from routing to cover, and **barks were chosen after the fact** to explain intent ("I need reinforcements" had no mechanism behind it, yet players read the next wave as reinforcements) ([Orkin, GDC 2006](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs540/handouts/gdc2006_orkin_jeff_fear.pdf)). **Ready or Not** shows the failure mode. Squads that "flank and keep corners covered" lost all credibility once they tracked players "through the wall, perfectly aiming at their heads": "you quickly realize that they're just cheating" ([Sports Illustrated Games](https://videogames.si.com/reviews/ready-or-not-review)).

Cover from a top-down view is thinly sourced. **The Ascent** uses crouch-behind-low-cover plus an explicit "Aim High" input to shoot over low obstacles for extra stagger ([Fextralife wiki](https://theascent.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)). In **Intravenous**, crawling behind low obstacles keeps you under enemy sight lines, and lights can be shot out ([Medium review](https://medium.com/far-from-professional/intravenous-a-love-letter-to-tactical-stealth-action-44de7664bae8)). For feedback, Vlambeer recommends screen shake slightly opposite gunfire ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)). Alien: Isolation's flamethrower response is "it fled" rather than an HP bar.

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

Turn caps, aim settle and cover with height are RE4's bargain in top-down form, and they need RE4's other half. **The acid stalker must close in and surround, not outrun the player's turn rate, and its lunge wind-up must be long enough for a capped turn to finish if the player reacts to the tell.** Give the dash, dodge and lunge each a distinct audio-visual wind-up, with the lunge longest. Make every limit visible: an aim cone that visibly shrinks as aim settles, a reload progress cue, a stagger recovery cue. Use a short buffer that holds one command, newest input wins. Make reload cancellable only by the panic action, at a cost such as losing the magazine. Put turn rate, settle and ready time into each weapon's entry in `data/*.json`, so weight becomes a loadout choice. Give the stalker an Alien: Isolation menace gauge that uses the crew's maximum and forces a retreat into vents at its peak. A turn-capped player with no recovery window is a player being farmed, not scared. Its senses should make the flashlight a beacon, which turns "flashlight = sight" into a real trade-off. Escalate by frequency (L4D), spawn from behind and from unlit areas, and consider "learned" escalation keyed to how the crew keeps escaping. Build pirate squads from two to four F.E.A.R.-style behaviours on cover-node lists, have them bark before acting ("Pinning them!", "Reloading!") as their wind-up, and have them visibly obey the same turn and settle rules. Their perception must be limited by light and line of sight. One case of shooting through walls costs more credibility than any tactic earns. Make low cover readable from above with a distinct top face, a visible crouch pose on the rigged player, and impact puffs on the cover lip. Treat hard snap-to-cover as unproven at this camera angle: a soft automatic brace or an Ascent-style aim-high input is cheaper to A/B. Keep screen shake small, directional and adjustable, and save hit-stop for lunge contact and knockdowns, because shake competes with reading the aim cone.

## Art: constraints that hide low fidelity beat ambition

### What the sources show

The small teams that achieved a strong look chose a constraint that flatters cheap assets. A high top-down camera "hides low-resolution textures" and flatters effects such as fire ([Waltorious](https://waltoriouswritesaboutgames.com/2015/09/27/history-lessons-shadowgrounds/)). Darkwood's three developers leaned on darkness and desaturation ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-darkwood-i-s-visibility-mechanics-create-a-new-kind-of-horror)). Teleglitch used abstract black shadow shapes. **Neon Giant built The Ascent with 11 people** around "We can compete in quality, not in scope", one art director's style (neon, noir, metallic), and procedural tools for smoke and destruction. This comes from a mirror of an Unreal Engine interview, because the original failed to load ([mirror](https://goodwinhatiou.blogspot.com/2022/03/the-ascent-interview-how-neon-giant-is.html)). Diablo IV chose a small set of signature features to polish (volumetric dust, emissive interactables, a custom tone curve) and added a calibration screen ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/fr-fr/article/23964183/sous-le-vernis-les-graphismes-de-diablo-iv)). The repeated art failures are **repetitive corridors**: Alien Breed Evolution ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/alien-breed-evolution-review/1900-6244192/)), late Shadowgrounds, Ruiner's layouts, Intravenous's "recycled" look ([KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/intravenous-review-splinter-cell-miami/)). Enemies that blend into the background palette are the other. Alien Breed's brief views of space outside the ship were singled out as welcome relief.

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

For stage 3, the evidence argues for **one key light, one palette, one or two signature effects**. The flashlight's volumetric beam plus steam or sparks would unify kit-built interiors and AI-generated props, and the 60° camera will hide the texture flaws generated assets usually have. Set a value-contrast rule before making assets: environments desaturated and mid-to-low value, enemies always carrying a saturated accent or rim light, and industrial clutter kept off the walkable floor plane so silhouettes read from above. This is the direct fix for the Intravenous and Ruiner complaints. Standee-sprite enemies (the project's art rule) can show stagger and knockdown as cheap pose swaps, which also serves RE4-style readable hit reactions. Plan **landmark set-pieces** per hull type (a viewport onto space, a reactor hall, a cargo cathedral) as relief from corridors, because repetition is the most common art criticism in this genre. Ship a brightness and black-point calibration screen, scalable text and colourblind-safe hazard colours from the start. Darkwood and Synthetik lost points for exactly these, and they are cheap.

## Progression: the quota is the game, meta is garnish

### What the sources show

The friend-group hits share one structure. **Lethal Company** runs rising profit quotas on a three-day cycle. Missing one ejects the crew and restarts everything. Within a dive, the ship leaves at midnight, and if no living player is aboard, all collected scrap is lost. Inventory holds four scrap items ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)). **Content Warning** gives three days to hit a view quota, one dive per day. Underground oxygen drains over about 500 seconds and stops inside the diving bell. The loot is literally footage of how close you got to the monsters. Five developers built it in a roughly two-month jam ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_Warning_(video_game))). **R.E.P.O.** has physics valuables that **lose value when damaged** and a between-level shop of upgrades that last for the run ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.E.P.O.)). **Risk of Rain 2** makes time the explicit difficulty driver on a visible bar with named tiers that escalate to "HAHAHAHA", and more players speed up the clock ([RoR2 wiki](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Difficulty)). **Hunt: Showdown** makes the reward reveal your position ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/04/06/from-tarkov-to-arc-raiders-the-evolving-landscape-of-extraction-shooters)), the purest form of greed as a clock.

On loss, the friend games keep it **shared by the crew and limited to the run**: Lethal loses scrap only if nobody makes it back. The hardcore PvP games use full loss but add softeners. Arc Raiders keeps a safe pocket ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARC_Raiders)) and offers free random loadouts that players use for casual runs, bringing their best gear only on squad raids "so a wipe doesn't hit one player alone" ([GFinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/how-arc-raiders-solves-extraction-shooter-gear-fear-with-free-loadouts)). Hunt permanently removes a health bar per down and makes **death cost nothing until bloodline level 11** ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/hunt-showdown-what-happens-when-you-die,850013)). The Cycle: Frontier's metrics improved once it ended mandatory wipes and added free loadouts, though too late to save it ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/yager-sunsetting-the-cycle-frontier-after-cheaters-cause-irreparable-damage)).

On meta-progression, the notable failures are instructive. **Darktide's** loot and crafting were called "a fantastic FPS ruined by a rubbish MMO". Players fell away by January 2023, and the CEO paused new content to fix progression ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhammer_40,000:_Darktide)). **Marathon's** three-month seasons reset most progress. Sales reportedly missed expectations, and a Forbes headline said Season 2 "did not turn things around" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_(2026_video_game))). Helldivers 2's CEO said progression must be "more than just grinding for XP" and declined a CoD-style prestige ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/helldivers-2-ceo-meaningful-progression-thats-more-than-just-grinding-for-xp)). Deep Rock Galactic made cosmetics a pillar from day one and drove check-ins with free seasons and events ([Game Developer, GDC 2023](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2023/developing-a-live-game-that-never-truly-left-early-access-with-deep-rock-galactic)). Lethal Company has almost no persistent meta and still sold an estimated 10M+ ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_Company)). Its solo developer then burned out on updates ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/lethalcompany-20240831-308611/)). For variety, **Hardspace: Shipbreaker** found that hand-building every ship would cap the game at about 5–10 hours. It switched to "systems as content": pseudo-procedural modular ships plus elemental hazards ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/inside-the-twisting-turning-development-of-i-hardspace-shipbreaker-i-)). Arc Raiders varies fixed maps with **map conditions** that change raid length and rules.

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

"Greed is the clock" fits the proven frame closely. Use a **three-dive contract cycle with a rising Company repayment demand**, reset on failure, inside which each dive has a diegetic, visible clock with named tiers. Examples: reactor decay, a Company recall beacon, a spreading hull breach. Each tier is a "do we leave now?" moment, and the crew should be able to see what the next tier costs. Give the dive a **safe pocket** (the salvage tug, an airlock) where the timer pauses so the crew can count loot and argue, which is where friend-group fun happens. Add Hunt's twist: **high-value salvage that raises danger when lifted** (it pings pirates or wakes the stalker), so greed becomes a decision about each item, not only about when to leave. R.E.P.O.'s damage-sensitive valuables pair well with weighty movement. Make loss belong to the crew and to the run: a wiped crew loses the haul and possibly the cycle, a lone survivor dragging the haul home is the hero moment, and nobody loses personal build progress. Satirical softeners fit the tone and the evidence: one safe-pocket item, free "Company-issue" loadouts, "insurance" with a deductible, and downs that cost a run resource as in Hunt. Scale the clock and quota for solo play rather than letting more players speed it up the way RoR2 does. If you add meta, keep it cheap and safe: cosmetics and tug decoration, unlocks that widen choice instead of raw power, and a **crew-shared debt as the long arc**. The satirical prestige writes itself: "debt cleared, here's a bigger one." Avoid wipes, crafting RNG, level-cap treadmills, and personal power gaps that make the friend who plays most carry the others. For variety, combine faction × hull × hazard × modifier ("pirates already aboard", "AI awake") on handcrafted modules. Given Lethal's "alien zoo" approach and the solo developer's burnout, plan content as small data-driven additions, not big drops.

## Tone: satire that lives in the rules outlasts satire that talks

### What the sources show

**Hardspace: Shipbreaker** is the closest thematic precedent. Workers start in "a crippling pile of debt", tools are rented, supplies come from the company store, and death is "regenerated" for a fee. "The company is your family, even as you pay a premium for your own death". The satire rests on sincere coworker warmth (emails, the Hab, a union subplot) and treats the job as "good, honest work" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hardspace-shipbreaker-review)). Its team rejected both a slow cosmic-horror prototype and an arcade over-correction before settling on a grounded "blue-collar fantasy" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/inside-the-twisting-turning-development-of-i-hardspace-shipbreaker-i-)). **Lethal Company** treats the Company as little more than a premise and a quota. The horror is played straight ("incredibly terrifying"), and "every death is a setback" ([Gamecritics](https://gamecritics.com/gc-staff/lethal-company-review/)). The laughs come from proximity voice, absurd deaths and spectating, because horror and comedy share the same build-and-release structure ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/lethal-companys-co-op-horror-comedy-is-like-phasmophobia-but-immediately-more-chaotic-and-deadly)). COGconnected credits Lethal's success to moments where one friend's mistake gets another killed ([COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/feature/what-makes-lethal-company-so-good/)). **Helldivers 2's** propaganda gets sharper when it reacts to play, "commend[ing] their undeniable tactical brilliance" after a blunder (PC Gamer headline, [link](https://pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-players-endure-sarcastic-propaganda-that-commends-their-undeniable-tactical-brilliance-after-the-blob-abandons-a-nearly-won-objective-for-basically-no-reason)).

The failures are about volume and teeth. **High on Life's** guns "talk your ears off", repeat "the same basic gag", and even complain about disuse right after use ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/high-on-life/low-on-laughs)). **DRG's** Scout grapple lines were "stamped out after the first ten times", and the developer conceded "the limit was set too low" ([devtrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/deep-rock-galactic/p/e64b9bd1-does-anyone-else-feel-that-the-new-scout-lines-are-seriously-out-of-place-gsg-is-trying-to-make-the-game-funny-and-light-hearted-and-it-doesn-t-work)). **Borderlands'** own art director called the second game "a little meme-y and silly" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/comedy-borderlands-biggest-pitfall/)). **The Outer Worlds 2** drew a PC Gamer critique of corporate satire too cartoonish to have "fury" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/the-outer-worlds-2s-toothless-satire-of-capitalism-should-be-a-disappointment-to-everyone-except-microsoft)), although other outlets were positive. Valve's tools address the budget problem. Wolpaw found that "if we added more of it, people just tuned out more" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/still-alive-kim-swift-and-erik-wolpaw-talk-portal)). Valve's dynamic dialogue system picks the most specific matching rule, writes "played" facts back with auto-expiry, gates lines randomly, and re-checks context before follow-ups ([Valve GDC 2012](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2012/GDC2012_Ruskin_Elan_DynamicDialog.pdf)).

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

The rental suit HUD, payslip and death bill are already the right surfaces, because they are the institution speaking in a straight voice, which is where satire held up in every sourced case. The rules: **the satire is loud outside danger and nearly silent inside it.** The HUD stays terse and clinical ("O2: 4 min. Billing continues."), never quips in combat or at the moment of death, and the death bill arrives afterwards as the release. Monsters and pirates are never the joke. The Company is. **Every fee should change a decision.** Oxygen billing pushes speed, the death bill pushes caution, insurance tiers are a real choice. A fee that is pure flavour becomes a written joke and wears out like one. Make the text **run-specific and data-driven**: itemised death-bill lines per cause of death, deductions for actual mistakes, and fine-print clauses that reveal real modifiers. Store it in JSON with `weight`, `cooldown`, `requires` and `played` fields as a lightweight version of Valve's system, and prefer printed text over voice, since documents can be skimmed and don't nag. Keep fees plausible, like real airline or gig-app fees, not cartoonish. Joke about the paperwork for a death, never the dead. Borrow Hardspace's counterweight if any narrative layer arrives: a coworker or crew channel that is sincere, so the satire has someone to hurt.

## Multiplayer: play with friends, not strangers, and structure for it now

### What the sources show

Friend co-op sticks when **communication is a mechanic** (Lethal's walkie-talkies to the "man in the chair" on the ship), the group shares **one pass/fail goal**, and **dead players stay entertained** in dead chat or spectating ([COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/feature/what-makes-lethal-company-so-good/)). Barotrauma made its Traitor mode an optional server toggle ([Barotrauma](https://barotraumagame.com/updates/karma-traitors-update-with-easier-server-hosting)). PvPvE is the cautionary tale. **The Cycle: Frontier** shut down in September 2023 after early cheating caused a "significant decrease in our player base" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/yager-sunsetting-the-cycle-frontier-after-cheaters-cause-irreparable-damage)). **Splitgate 2** "unlaunched" after its founders admitted launching too early with too much scope ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/splitgate-2-is-unlaunching-as-developer-1047-games-cuts-staff/)). Even in the hit Arc Raiders, **about 30% of players focus on PvE**, which "caught us a little bit by surprise", and shared PvE threats like the Matriarch got strangers to stop fighting within seconds ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/about-30-percent-of-arc-raiders-players-focus-on-pve-and-embarks-happy-with-that-we-want-to-instil-hope-in-the-player/)).

Technically, Godot's high-level API uses ENet by default. "The server's ID is always 1." A host who also plays needs `call_local` on RPCs that must run locally. Continuous state should use unreliable channels. Internet play over raw ENet needs port forwarding ([Godot docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.rst)). **GodotSteam's SteamMultiplayerPeer** connects by Steam ID, works with standard spawners and synchronizers, needs authority set in `_enter_tree()`, and leaves host migration to the developer ([GodotSteam](https://godotsteam.com/tutorials/multiplayer_peer/)). Common desyncs come from inconsistent authority, mismatched synchronizer paths that fail silently, and nodes spawned on only one peer ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-godot-multiplayer-authority-desync)). **R.E.P.O.'s** host-only physics authority makes clients wait "a full round trip delay" to grab objects, which a community mod tries to fix ([NetworkingReworked README](https://gh.r-mj.com/Ian0526/NetworkingReworked)). Practitioner writing describes retrofitting multiplayer as costly ([coherence](https://coherence.io/blog/tech-demo/hellfront-retrofitting-multiplayer-using-coherence)), but this is anecdotal and from sources not read in full.

### What it means for Grave Rats (inference)

The "rival pirate squads" should stay **AI**. Strangers-PvP brings matchmaking, anti-cheat and population needs that killed better-funded games. If friend-versus-friend rivalry ever appeals, make it a private-lobby mode (say 2v2 crews on one derelict), where cheating is irrelevant. The top-down view lends itself to Lethal's "man in the chair": one player on the tug's console sees the derelict map, door states and tracker blips, and the same seat gives a dead player a job. That information asymmetry works over the Monday crew's Discord without in-game proximity voice. For the stack, use a **host-authoritative listen server**: ENet during development, SteamMultiplayerPeer at release, no dedicated servers, and a clean "host left" screen instead of migration. Given R.E.P.O.'s lesson, give each client authority over its own movement while the host owns damage, loot, doors and AI. Among friends, that trade costs nothing. Real lag compensation is probably overkill at friend-group pings.

## Gaps and confidence

Several comparables could not be sourced and are kept out of the findings above. These include Darkwood's threat design, Hunt and Tarkov handling and inertia (the closest "weight" references), Door Kickers, Monaco and Alien Swarm camera specifics, DRG's class philosophy and Deep Dive structure, Barotrauma, Abiotic Factor, and Fallout and R.E.P.O.'s tone. Hardspace's later no-timer "Open Shift" mode, often cited as a response to timer stress, was not verified and is left out. No hard retention data (D30 curves), no developer talk comparing systemic and written humour head-to-head, no source on motion sickness from rotating cones, no numbers for hit-stop or shake, and no Godot performance data for shadow-heavy top-down games were found. The claim that retrofitting multiplayer is expensive rests on practitioner titles and a vendor case study, not on measured cost. Some figures are secondary or conflicting: R.E.P.O.'s peak player count is flagged on Wikipedia, Marathon's sales reports conflict (about 1.2M copies versus 2.2M players), and the Helldivers ergonomics description comes from a guide site. The most valuable follow-ups would be Hunt and Tarkov weapon-handling analyses, cover readability talks from XCOM or Gears, and a Godot frame-time test of the flashlight-occluder approach on John's Mac.

## Conclusion

Seen across these games, Grave Rats' riskiest pillar is not any single mechanic but the way its pillars interact. Weighty controls, flashlight-as-sight and a hunting stalker each work in isolation in some comparable game. Stacked together, they multiply the ways a player can die without understanding why. The unifying design principle is therefore **legibility of cause**. Every limit is shown, every attacker reveals itself, every threat backs off on a schedule, and every fee on the death bill names exactly what you did. That same principle makes the satire work, because a payslip is only funny when it is precisely, unfairly *yours*.

The second implication concerns scope. The games that hit with friend groups were made by one to five people and carried almost no meta-progression or live service, while the AAA extraction games that stumbled did so on population, cheating and wipes, problems Grave Rats can choose never to have. The best plan is therefore smaller than the genre's reputation suggests. Polish one three-dive contract cycle with a clock you can read, a stalker that retreats, pirates that announce themselves, and a Company that bills you for all of it. Then add co-op on a structure laid down before the art makes refactoring expensive.
